# Campaign Bible (Core): Graymere County

## 1) Setting overview
**Kingdom:** Valewyr
- **Capital:** Asterhall
- **Ruling house:** House Cauldrin
- **King:** Odran III Cauldrin
- **Princess:** Maeryn Cauldrin

**County:** Graymere
- **Seat:** Castle Harrowgate (old border-fort turned county seat)
- **Market town:** Eastbridge (stone bridge crossing and riverside market)
- **Holdings:** 1 town, **eight villages**, several hamlets and crofts

**Premise:** Ben Paran, newly made Count of Graymere, inherits a neglected county after the prior count’s execution for treason (details unknown to Ben at start). The realm is broadly at peace and prosperous—but Graymere looks tired, under-kept, and politically exposed.

## 2) Tone and playstyle
- **Realistic medieval pressures:** hunger, weather, debt, manpower limits.
- **Intrigue-forward:** information is power; loyalty is negotiated.
- **Lethal violence:** fights are consequential; armor, ambush, and morale matter.
- **Long-running sandbox:** no planned end; the county and realm evolve.

## 3) Canon constraints
- **No magic.**
- **No Christian church.** Religion is local/customary (shrines, seasonal rites, oath traditions), not a unified political institution.
- **Nobles rule:** law and land tenure are feudal; offices are personal, not bureaucratic.

## 4) Key starting characters (public knowledge)
- **PC:** Count Ben Paran of Graymere (newly elevated; high justice by royal charter)
- **Companion:** Tomas Rell (childhood friend; ex-guard; practical soldier)
- **Caretaker-castellan (likely):** Garrick Holt (“Old Holt”), keeper of keys and whatever order remains

## 5) Governance & law (your authority)
- **High Justice:** you may try and execute, seize property, and issue judgments within the county.
- **Levy rights:** you may raise levies, but doing so reduces **Labor** and risks unrest.
- **Fortification license:** you may repair/build fortifications.

**Reality check:** authority is only as strong as your ability to enforce it—garrison size, legitimacy, and local cooperation.

## 6) Domain management system (medium)
### 6.1 Resources tracked
- **Silver (coin):** in Crowns/Stags/Penns
- **Stores:** food measured in **weeks of supply** for household + garrison
- **Labor:** abstracted workforce capacity (0–100). Levies and major projects reduce it.
- **Order:** 0–6
  - 0–1: lawless / open defiance
  - 2–3: unstable / banditry and bribery common
  - 4: functional
  - 5: orderly
  - 6: exceptionally loyal and compliant

### 6.2 Currency
- **1 Crown (cr) = 20 Stags (st)**
- **1 Stag (st) = 12 Penns (pn)**

Benchmarks (typical, varies by scarcity):
- Bread loaf: 1–2 pn
- Ale: 2–3 pn
- Unskilled day labor: 6–10 pn
- Skilled day labor: 1–2 st
- Spear: 3–5 st
- Mail shirt: 25–60 cr

### 6.3 Time
- **Day-by-day scenes.**
- **Time advances only when Ben sleeps** (player says “I go to bed”).
- **Weekly tick (every 7 nights slept):** routine wages/consumption, small developments.
- **Seasonal tick (every 90 nights slept):** tax/harvest cycle, major project steps, political shifts.

### 6.4 Revenues (categories)
- **Town dues:** tolls, market fees, craft licenses, small fines (monthly tendency)
- **Village renders:** coin + produce equivalents (seasonal)
- **Demesne output:** revenue from your directly-managed lands (seasonal)
- **Justice income:** fines, confiscations (irregular; impacts Order)

### 6.5 Expenditures (categories)
- **Garrison/household wages** (if hired/kept)
- **Stores consumption** (mouths eat; winter is harsh)
- **Maintenance & repairs** (neglect compounds)
- **Administration** (reeves, scribes, messengers)
- **Obligations** (hosting, gifts, travel, feasts)
- **Relief** (grain purchases, rebuilding after fires)

### 6.6 Crown obligations
- **Crown Tax:** expected **110 Crowns/year**.
  - Paid as **55 cr midsummer** and **55 cr mid-autumn** (default). Terms may be renegotiated via favors or politics.

## 7) Military model
- **Household men / garrison:** small professional core (dozens, not hundreds).
- **Levies:** short-term manpower drawn from villages/town; reduces **Labor** and can lower **Order**.
- **Warfare:** supply and morale decide outcomes as much as steel.

## 8) Geography (what’s known at start)
- Graymere is a **river-and-marsh county** with an important **bridge crossing** at Eastbridge.
- Late winter travel is slow; thaw brings floods and bandit opportunity.

## 9) Themes to lean on
- Legitimacy vs fear
- Justice vs revenue
- Oaths vs self-interest
- The cost of readiness (soldiers eat; walls cost)
- Information asymmetry (you start ignorant; others are not)
