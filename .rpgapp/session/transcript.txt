- The kitchen is already working. You hear it before you see it: the scrape of a pot, the hush of someone moving quietly so they don’t wake those who can still sleep.
- **Firewood** is treated like coin. Whoever draws it does so with care—small, measured loads.
- The **guards** change on and off the wallwalk in pairs or singles (because there are only four to cover everything). It’s not “drill,” it’s simply staying awake and keeping eyes open.

**Early morning**
- The courtyard begins to move: a bucket line to the well/cistern, someone hauling a sack from stores, a quick sweep of the hall where mud gets tracked in.
- The **stables** are a choke point. If the horses aren’t fed and watered, nothing goes anywhere. **Jory** has been making hay stretch; you can see it in how he portions it out—careful, almost apologetic.
- The **armory** isn’t busy, but it’s present in everyone’s mind: a few serviceable spears, battered shields, repaired mail—enough to look like a household that can defend itself, not enough to feel secure.

**Midmorning**
- This is when decisions *want* to happen because it’s when people are upright and close enough to speak.
- **Holt** will be doing his quiet circuit—keys, doors, stores, checks—because he’s the only real “system” Harrowgate has left.
- If you call the household to the hall again, they’ll come; they’re not lazy, just worn. They’re watching you to see whether you’re another short-lived lord… or the one who stays.

**Noon meal**
- It’s plain and warm rather than plentiful: broth, coarse bread, maybe a bit of salt pork if there’s any to spare.
- This is when grievances or worries tend to surface *indirectly*—a look between the guards, Anwen pausing before she answers, Bram mentioning a broken hinge like it’s just a hinge (and not the hundredth small thing that’s gone without proper repair).

**Afternoon**
- Small maintenance happens if it can: patching tack, mending a strap, tightening a gate chain, cleaning a spearhead.
- If you intend to send riders or receive anyone from Eastbridge, afternoon is often the practical time to do it—light enough to travel, not yet the cold fall of evening.

**Evening**
- The castle draws inward. Doors are barred earlier than you’d expect in a “peaceful” realm—part habit, part caution.
- Lamp light is rationed. You feel the lack of **lamp oil** not as a line item, but as darkness in corridors and the way people avoid unnecessary movement.

### What *this* specific day feels like
It’s your first stretch of time where the initial introductions are done and the castle has settled into the reality that you are actually here, awake, and likely to start asking questions.

There’s no trumpet-call urgency, but there’s pressure in the background:
- **Unpaid wages** hanging over every interaction like weather.
- **Eastbridge** as the county’s cleanest coin source—close enough to matter, far enough to be uncertain.
- **Thaw-season trouble** (Hask’s words) — the sort of trouble that comes when roads soften, people move again, and the watch is thin.
